\subsection{Fail2Drive pairs}
\label{sec:fail2drive}

Fail2Drive~\cite{gerstenecker2026fail2drive} pairs each \emph{generalisation}
route with a \emph{base} twin on the same road; only the targeted shift differs.
% published: 100 pairs, 17 scenario types (CITATION_AUDIT.md, fail2drive row)
There are 100 pairs over 17 scenario types (``types'' below). The benchmark
forbids training on its routes, scenarios or assets; we train nothing on it and
use it only to drive and score. A type's \emph{slice} is its generalisation
routes, and its \emph{neighbourhood} is their base twins. A \emph{unit} is one
(model, type) pair. Route ids encode the traffic seed
(\code{seed * 100000 + id}), so each seed's copy of a route is a separate route.

\paragraph{Gap classification.}
Each unit is classified on the model's own test pool, in this order
(\code{docs/prereg-fail2drive.md} \S1):
% design: thresholds from docs/prereg-fail2drive.md §1 (PRD.md §9.1)
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Gap}: at least 30 slice runs, a failure-rate uplift of at least
        20 points over the neighbourhood, a ratio of at least $2\times$, and
        Fisher $p < 0.01$. The paired McNemar test is reported beside it.
  % design: 95% and 20% (docs/prereg-fail2drive.md §1)
  \item \textbf{Ceiling}: a gap whose slice fails at least 95\% of the time and
        whose neighbourhood fails at most 20\%. It is a control, not a target.
  \item \textbf{Decoy}: $|\text{uplift}| \le 10$ points and McNemar $p > 0.2$.
        A decoy looks novel but is handled fine.
  \item \textbf{Background}: everything else. It stays in the pool and is never
        scored.
\end{enumerate}
% design: 12 factors, Jaccard 0.4 (configs/baselines/b4_factors_v1.yaml, tag b4-factors-v1)
A gap is \emph{B4-unnamed} if no single level of our enumerated factor list (B4,
12 factors, tagged \code{b4-factors-v1} before the Fail2Drive track existed) has a
failing set with Jaccard at least 0.4 against the gap's failing set. Otherwise it
is B4-named. The paper uses the name ``B4-unnamed'', never ``interactional''.

\paragraph{Rule units for the views comparison.}
The later views pre-registration (\S\ref{sec:prereg-views}) adds a looser unit
rule, \code{units\_rule.py}, run inside each read.
% design: 1.5x and at least 5 failing routes (docs/prereg-views.md, "Units, metric and intervals")
It admits every type whose generalisation routes fail at least $1.5\times$ as
often as their base twins, with at least 5 failing generalisation routes. The
primary units are those minus Wall, which is excluded by name because it was a
ceiling control before the sprint.

\paragraph{TransFuser++ as the first held-out model, then a second dev model.}
% design: 1,200 routes per model (CONFLICTS.md C38, Chris's scoping)
TransFuser++~\cite{jaeger2023hidden,zimmerlin2024hidden} was driven on the same
1,200-route design.
% measured: TF++ gap classification (docs/prd-discovery-sprint.md §2; transfer-spec.md item 7)
Its gaps under the pre-registered rule are FullyBlocked and
PedestriansOnRoad (\code{transfer-spec.md}, item 7). Both are SimLingo gaps too,
so it tests transfer across models, not across failure types. In round 1 it was
held out and scored once. Amendment A3 fixed in advance that if the primary
finalist's interval against B3-behaviour on TransFuser++ included zero,
TransFuser++ would become a second dev pool for a round-2 search, confirmed on
UniAD alone (\S\ref{sec:prereg-a3}). Round 2 was opened under that rule and
closed by A4. TransFuser++ was later re-driven to save camera frames, which only
that re-drive has.

\paragraph{UniAD, the held-out model.}
UniAD, in the Bench2DriveZoo release, is a modular camera-only model. It is not
cited here because no audited reference for it exists yet.
% design: six seeds 101-106, 1,200 routes (docs/prereg-views.md, Data)
Its test pool is the same six-seed design, 1,200 routes. No method was tuned on its
outcomes; they were read twice, an interim read and the final read, both fixed
before the tag (\S\ref{sec:prereg-views}). Section~\ref{sec:results} reports
the final read, the result of record.

\subsection{What a method sees}

% measured: frames are 5 sim steps apart on all three models (WHAT_BROKE.md 2026-11-16; transfer-spec.md table)
The capture agent records, every 5 simulation steps, mean-pooled activations at
named sites.
At 20\,Hz that is one frame every 0.25\,s. On SimLingo the sites are
\code{driving\_queries} (the query tokens the controller reads), \code{all\_tokens}
and \code{vision\_bridge}.
% design: SimLingo 24 x 896; TF++ 6 x 256 measured on one shard (transfer-spec.md table)
SimLingo's query site has 24 layers of width 896; TransFuser++'s has 6 of width
256. Methods also see ego speed per frame, the infraction counts and the
pass/fail outcome of each route. Before a method sees a shard, the fields
\code{scenario}, \code{practice\_gaps}, \code{role}, \code{neighbourhood\_of},
\code{base\_id}, \code{pair}, \code{seed}, \code{source} and the route id are
removed, and routes are renamed by a salted hash whose salt stays with the
scorer (\code{docs/prereg-fail2drive.md} \S3).
